## Post 25 - Dead Subplot Detection

**Topic:** Platform feature + craft
**Best day to post:** Monday

### LinkedIn

A dead subplot is one of the quieter ways a novel loses momentum.

You introduced a secondary character with a conflict. They showed up in four chapters. Had good scenes. And then nothing. The arc just stops. Not resolved, not intentionally abandoned, just forgotten.

By the time you reach the end of a draft, there are usually two or three of these. They are hard to catch because they were not failures in the moment. They were active threads that got crowded out by the main story.

The way to catch them: read your manuscript looking specifically for characters and plotlines that disappear. Not for content or prose. Just for threads.

Make a list of everyone who had a meaningful scene. Note the last chapter they appeared in. Ask: did they resolve? Did they need to? If the answer is no and no, you have a dead subplot.

They are almost always fixable. Sometimes one scene in the third act is all it needs. But first you have to find them.

## Post 26 - Semantic Search in Manuscripts

**Topic:** Feature education
**Best day to post:** Wednesday

### LinkedIn

One of the features we built that writers use more than we expected: semantic search across the manuscript.

Standard search finds the exact word or phrase. It is useful for finding instances of a character name or locating a specific scene.

Semantic search finds the meaning. You type "the moment Marcus loses hope" and it returns the passages where that happens, even if those words never appear.

This turns out to be surprisingly useful mid-draft.

"Find every scene where the protagonist is passive" catches something that keyword search never could. So does "scenes where the antagonist shows vulnerability" or "moments of transition in setting."

The manuscript becomes a searchable document of its own meaning, not just its words. Which means you can ask questions of your own story and get real answers.

## Post 27 - Global Replace is Not Find and Replace

**Topic:** Feature deep dive
**Best day to post:** Friday

### LinkedIn

When writers hear "global replace," they think: fancy find and replace.

It is not.

Find and replace operates on strings. It matches characters in sequence and swaps them.

A rename that actually works for fiction needs to understand:

- That "Marcus" in dialogue is a different thing from "Marcus" in narration
- That "Marcus's" is not the same match as "Marcus"
- That a character who narrates in first person still needs their name replaced in scenes where they are referred to in third
- That pronoun chains need to follow the character even when their name is not present

We spent several weeks on this feature. The engineering is not glamorous. It is mostly edge cases and tests. But it is the kind of feature where getting it 95 percent right is not good enough. The 5 percent that slips through is what the writer's editor finds.

The goal was: make it feel effortless. Hide all of this.

### X

Find and replace operates on strings.

A rename that works for fiction needs to understand grammar.

"Marcus" in dialogue vs narration. Possessives. First-person narrators. Pronoun chains.

95% right isn't good enough. The 5% is what the editor finds.

## Post 28 - Brainstorming With AI

**Topic:** How to use AI well
**Best day to post:** Tuesday

### LinkedIn

The most useful thing AI can do for a stuck writer is not write for them.

It is ask the right questions.

When you are stuck on a scene or plot problem, the instinct is to ask the AI what should happen next. Sometimes that is useful. More often it produces technically valid suggestions that do not feel like your story.

The better approach: use the AI to pressure-test your thinking.

"I am planning to have Marcus betray Elena in chapter 18. Here is their relationship so far. Does that feel earned?"

"I want the climax to involve the object from chapter 3. Here is where it has been mentioned. Does the setup support it?"

"My antagonist has been doing X throughout. I need them to do Y. What would have to be true for that to be consistent?"

These questions generate responses that help you think. You are still making the decisions. The AI is just refusing to let you skip the reasoning.

### X

The most useful thing AI can do for a stuck writer is not write for them.

It's ask the right questions.

"Does Marcus's betrayal feel earned given their history?"

"Does the setup in chapter 3 support this climax?"

You're still making the decisions. The AI is refusing to let you skip the reasoning.

## Post 29 - Long Form vs Short Form

**Topic:** Craft differences
**Best day to post:** Thursday

### LinkedIn

Short form and long form fiction share a lot of the same craft fundamentals.

But long form has problems that do not exist at the short end.

Character consistency across time. Plot threads that span 300 pages. Pacing at scale, where a chapter that works perfectly in isolation might slow the momentum of the book as a whole. Subplots that need to develop independently and also interact with the main story. World rules that have to stay internally consistent over years of reading.

The feedback loops are different too. With a short story, you finish a draft in a week and have something you can show people. With a novel, you are working for a year before you have a complete draft to react to.

This is why most writing advice breaks down for novelists. It is calibrated for shorter work. The techniques that help you write a good essay or a good short story are necessary but not sufficient.

If you are a novelist, the specific things you need help with are specific to the form. Seek out advice from people who have actually written long.

## Post 30 - For Writers Who Are Stuck

**Topic:** Encouragement + craft
**Best day to post:** Saturday

### LinkedIn

If you are stuck on your novel right now, this is for you.

The draft you have is not evidence of your limitations. It is evidence that you started. That you are in the middle of something genuinely difficult. That you got further than most people who wanted to write a novel ever get.

The chapters that feel wrong can be fixed. The character that is not working yet will work when you understand them better. The plot that tangles in the third act is a craft problem, and craft problems have solutions.

Being stuck is not a message from the universe that you should not be writing this. It is a message that you have reached the edge of what you know how to do so far. Which means you are exactly where you need to be to learn something.

Write the next scene even if it is bad. Finish the draft even if it is a mess.

The only novel that cannot be made better is the one that does not exist yet.
