If you write horror, grimdark fantasy, dark literary fiction, or fiction involving violence, trauma, or mature themes, you have probably already run into this problem.

You are mid-scene. Something visceral and necessary is happening. You ask your AI writing tool for a continuation or a suggestion, and it refuses. Or it softens the scene into something bloodless. Or it gives you a warning. Or, in Sudowrite's recent case, it flags your account.

This is not a fringe experience anymore. As AI writing tools have scaled and faced pressure around content moderation, many have quietly moved their models in a direction that makes them less useful for significant portions of literary fiction. Horror novelists and dark fiction writers are the most directly affected, but the problem is broader than genre. Any fiction that deals honestly with violence, sexuality, moral ambiguity, psychological disturbance, or trauma runs into these restrictions eventually.

Here is an honest assessment of the current landscape.

## Why the Problem Got Worse

The tools that writers rely on most — Sudowrite in particular — have migrated their underlying models toward Claude and similar APIs with built-in content policies. These policies are designed for general use cases and are not calibrated for literary fiction's specific needs.

Horror as a genre has always explored things that are uncomfortable by design. The Shining is psychologically violent. Blood Meridian is graphically brutal. House of Leaves is deeply disturbing. These are canonical works. A writing tool that cannot assist with fiction at this level of darkness is not a serious tool for serious writers in these genres.

This is not a philosophical point. It is a practical one. If your tool refuses to help you write a scene your novel requires, it is not a writing tool for your novel.

## The Tools That Work

### NovelAI

NovelAI is the most permissive option currently available at scale. It runs fine-tuned models with minimal content filtering, and its Lorebook system allows persistent character and world memory across sessions.

The honest tradeoff: prose quality. NovelAI's models are designed for volume and flexibility, not for the sentence-level craft that makes literary horror work. The output can be workmanlike — functional but flat. Writers using it typically treat it as a drafting aid rather than a prose partner.

For writers who need zero guardrails above everything else, it is the current default choice. Pricing is straightforward: around $10–$25 a month depending on tier, no separate API costs.

### NovelCrafter with a Custom API Key

NovelCrafter's bring-your-own-key model means that content restrictions are governed by whichever model you connect to it, not by NovelCrafter itself. If you use a model with more permissive settings, you inherit that model's content latitude.

The friction is real: setting up and managing API keys is not a natural workflow for writers. You also pay the tool subscription and the API costs separately, which makes the effective price less predictable. But for writers who are comfortable with the technical side, this approach gives access to NovelCrafter's strong worldbuilding tools without Sudowrite's content restrictions.

### Local Models via LM Studio or Ollama

Writers with capable hardware are running local language models via tools like LM Studio or Ollama. Local models have no content restrictions because there is no API — the model runs entirely on your own machine. Several open-source models (Mistral, LLaMA-based fine-tunes) are specifically trained on fiction and perform well for drafting tasks.

The barrier is hardware: you need a GPU with significant VRAM for models large enough to produce quality output. This is not an option for everyone, but for writers who have the setup, it is the most freedom you can get.

## What to Avoid

**Do not try to work around content restrictions by rephrasing or obscuring your intent.** This is a waste of time and produces worse output. If a tool is not designed to work with your fiction, the solution is to use a different tool, not to find ways to fool the existing one.

**Do not use ChatGPT or Claude directly for long-form fiction assistance.** These are general tools with general content policies. They will also forget your characters, your world, and your established plot logic the moment a conversation gets long enough. They are not designed for novels, and they perform accordingly.

**Do not compromise the fiction.** This sounds obvious, but writers who have been burned by content restrictions sometimes start making choices about their manuscripts based on what their tool will allow rather than what the story requires. That is the wrong direction. The tool should serve the story.

## The Honest Bottom Line

If you write dark fiction and your current tool is failing you, the problem is the tool, not the genre.

Horror, grimdark, dark literary fiction, and mature content fiction have a readership, a tradition, and craft requirements as rigorous as any other form. The writing tools that work for these genres are the ones built with that understanding.

The options above are imperfect, but they are honest. Find the one whose tradeoffs you can work with, test it on scenes your current tool fails at, and make the decision based on actual results.

Your fiction deserves a tool that can follow you into the dark.
